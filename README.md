This mini project presents an analytical and experimental study of the bias–variance trade-off in text classification models. Using the AG News corpus (120,000 training and 7,600 test news articles in four classes) and TF-IDF word uni-gram and bi-gram features, three classical classifiers – Logistic Regression, Linear Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Multinomial Naïve Bayes – were trained on randomly drawn subsets of ten different sizes, from 100 to 120,000 documents, with repeated resampling at each size. For every size, the training accuracy, test accuracy, generalisation gap, run-to-run standard deviation and a zero-one-loss bias–variance decomposition were computed.
The results show that the variance term is large for very small training sets (36.42% for Logistic Regression at 100 documents) and exceeds the bias term only up to about 200 documents; it then falls steadily to 2.08% at 50,000 documents, while the bias declines more slowly (from 20.92% to 7.57%) and becomes the limiting factor. Additional experiments that vary the regularisation strength and the vocabulary size reproduce the classical under-fitting to over-fitting behaviour. The best model, a Linear SVM trained on the full training set, reached 92.80% test accuracy (macro F1 = 92.79%), and Logistic Regression reached 92.43%, so the 90% accuracy target was achieved. The study uses only Python 3 with the scikit-learn, pandas, NumPy and Matplotlib libraries.
